About & Methodology

What this is

An independent, non-commercial index of information the All India Football Federation (AIFF) has already published on its own website (the-aiff.com), plus a hand-curated, cited timeline of governance events drawn from independent news reporting. It exists because AIFF's own site buries this material in nested pages that are hard to search, compare over time, or link to directly.

What this is not

  • Not affiliated with, endorsed by, or run by AIFF, FIFA, AFC, or any government body.
  • Not a news outlet — we don't reproduce full articles; every news entry links out to the original.
  • Not a claim of wrongdoing beyond what a cited, dated source already states. Allegations are labelled as allegations (see the status field on Timeline entries) — several are explicitly disputed or denied by the person involved, and we say so.
  • Not comprehensive. The scraper covers what AIFF's site structure allows us to parse automatically; some sub-sections aren't fully machine-readable yet (noted on the Documents page).

How the data is produced

Documents, News, Governance pages — refreshed automatically, daily, by a script that fetches AIFF's own public pages and extracts structured records (title, date/year, category, and a direct link to the original PDF or article). No text is invented or summarized by a model; it's parsed directly from AIFF's HTML. Every record carries a source_url and a scraped_at timestamp.

Timeline page — compiled by hand from published news reporting (Scroll.in, ESPN, Business Standard, EastMojo, The Week, Khel Now, Tribune India, The Bridge, and similar outlets — see each entry's own citations). Each entry links to the specific article(s) it's drawn from. This page is updated periodically as new, verifiable developments happen — not scraped automatically, precisely because judgment calls about what's newsworthy and how to phrase disputed claims need a human, not a bot.

Why AIFF is fair game for a public tracker

AIFF is recognised by the Government of India as the National Sports Federation for football, operates under a constitution that has been the subject of repeated Supreme Court intervention, and its accounts have been the subject of a CAG (Comptroller & Auditor General) audit process per contemporaneous news reporting. Bodies that draw on public recognition, public infrastructure and (per National Sports Federation norms) public funding are legitimately subject to public scrutiny — the same principle behind RTI applying to substantially government-funded bodies.

Corrections & feedback

Found a broken link, a mis-scraped title, an outdated timeline entry, or a factual error? This project is open-source — open an issue or pull request on the GitHub repository (link on the homepage once published), or reach the maintainer through the repository's contact details. Corrections are treated as seriously as new entries: getting AIFF's own numbers right is the entire point of this project.
