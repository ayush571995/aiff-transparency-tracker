What AIFF Is Actually Required To Do

AIFF isn't a private club — it's India's Government-recognised National Sports Federation (NSF) for football, which means specific statutory and regulatory obligations apply to how it's governed and how transparent it must be. This page lays out that framework, cited to primary/secondary sources, so claims of "mismanagement" can be checked against actual rules rather than vibes.

1. The legal framework, in order

National Sports Development Code of India, 2011 (NSDC)

The original administrative code governing NSFs. Key obligations it places on a federation like AIFF:

  • Elections: must be conducted per the federation's own constitution and government guidelines; improper conduct is grounds for suspension of recognition.
  • Tenure & age limits: President capped at 12 years total (with or without a break); Secretary/Treasurer limited to two consecutive 4-year terms with a mandatory 4-year cooling-off period after; all office bearers must vacate at age 70.
  • Financial management: proper accounting required, with annual audited accounts submitted by a registered Chartered Accountant. Failure to provide audited accounts is grounds for withdrawal of recognition.
  • RTI: NSFs receiving Rs. 10 lakh or more government funding in a financial year are declared "Public Authorities" and must comply with the Right to Information Act, 2005 — including designating a Central Public Information Officer (CPIO).
  • Consequences of non-compliance: recognition can be suspended or withdrawn, which also removes the right to select national teams, represent India internationally, or use the word "India" in the federation's name.

National Sports Governance Act, 2025 (effective 18 August 2025)

A new statutory law (not just an administrative code) that supersedes and strengthens the above. It creates:

  • National Sports Board (NSB): apex authority that grants/reviews federation recognition and maintains a compliance register.
  • National Sports Tribunal: dedicated dispute-resolution body chaired by a sitting/retired Chief Justice, with appeals going straight to the Supreme Court.
  • Mandatory internal bodies: every federation must run an Athletes Committee, an Ethics Committee, and a Dispute Resolution Committee.
  • Composition rules: Executive Committees capped at 15 members, including at least 2 elite athletes and 4 women.
  • Age/tenure: office bearers must be 25-70 years old (extendable to 75 where international federation rules require), with mandatory cooling-off periods.
  • Electoral oversight: a National Sports Election Panel supervises federation elections.
  • Transparency: government-funded federations are RTI public authorities; their accounts are subject to CAG audit with reports tabled in Parliament.
  • Athlete safety: a mandatory Safe Sports Policy and independent grievance mechanism for harassment/abuse complaints.

AIFF's own constitution (Supreme Court-approved, 19 September 2025)

AIFF's constitution itself — the document that governs its internal elections, committee structure and disqualification standards — has been rewritten under direct Supreme Court supervision since 2022. As approved:

  • 15 eminent former players added to the General Body (elected state associations retain over 62% control).
  • Player eligibility to stand for these seats: 5+ international caps (men), 2+ (women).
  • 3 Vice Presidents (up from 2), at least one must be a woman; Executive Committee capped at 15 members.
  • Disqualification standard tightened: mere "framing of charge" is no longer enough — now requires "conviction followed by imprisonment."
  • Constitution's application extended to state associations and local bodies, for FIFA-standard alignment.

3. Why RTI applies here

Under the NSDC 2011 (carried forward and strengthened by the NSGA 2025), any National Sports Federation receiving Rs. 10 lakh or more in government funding in a financial year is a "Public Authority" under Section 2(h) of the Right to Information Act, 2005, obligated to designate a CPIO and respond to information requests. AIFF's own audit reports confirm it receives government funding routed via the Sports Authority of India (SAI) — see the FY2022-23 entry on Financials. That is the direct legal basis for the RTI request generator on the Financials page.
