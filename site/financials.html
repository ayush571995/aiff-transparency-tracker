Financial Audits & Spending

What AIFF actually reported earning and spending, fiscal year by fiscal year (1 April – 31 March), straight from its own chartered-accountant-audited statements. AIFF does not publish month-by-month spending anywhere, and no statement for FY2025-26 exists yet — see the honesty note below before you read the table.

⚠️ What we can and can't show you

  • No month-by-month data exists. AIFF discloses finances annually, not monthly. Any site claiming a monthly AIFF spending chart is presenting numbers nobody has published.
  • Nothing for FY2025-26 (Apr 2025 – Mar 2026) is published yet. Audit reports typically land 6-10 months after fiscal year-end; the FY2024-25 report itself was only signed 30 Sep 2025, six months after that year closed.
  • Line items aren't published per-contract. AIFF's income/expenditure statement groups spending into a handful of categories (e.g. "Tournament camps and other expenses" — one number covering an entire year of activity). It does not disclose what was paid to which vendor, for what, or when.
  • What tenders/RFPs tell you (and what they don't): the "Where the money is directed" section below shows what AIFF is procuring — sourced from its own published tender documents — but AIFF generally does not publish the value it actually pays out per contract. Ask AIFF directly: RTI request generator

AIFF receives government recognition and funding and, per the National Sports Development Code 2011 and the National Sports Governance Act 2025, federations meeting the funding threshold are "public authorities" under the Right to Information Act, 2005 — meaning you can formally request records they haven't voluntarily published. Pick what you want to ask about; this generates a ready-to-send RTI application draft. See Conduct & Rules for the legal basis.
